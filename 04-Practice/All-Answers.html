Chapter 1 answer key

1.1 Overview of Anatomy and Physiology

  • A. Physiology, because the question asks how an organ functions.
  • B. The thin exchange barrier and large surface area help explain efficient gas diffusion.

1.2 Structural Organization of the Human Body

  • A. It contains multiple tissue types working together, including epithelium, connective tissue, smooth muscle, and nervous tissue.
  • B. The organ system.

1.3 Functions of Human Life

  • A. Anabolic: smaller glucose units are assembled into a larger storage molecule.
  • B. Cilia moving mucus, the heart pumping blood, or a cell moving vesicles internally.

1.4 Requirements for Human Life

  • A. It can alter protein structure and interfere with enzyme activity and other cell functions.
  • B. Lower atmospheric pressure reduces oxygen partial pressure, reducing the driving force for oxygen uptake.

1.5 Homeostasis

  • A. Cooling reduces the rise in temperature that triggered sweating.
  • B. Delivery removes the cervical-stretch stimulus, ending that reinforcing loop.

1.6 Anatomical Terminology

  • A. Lateral.
  • B. The visceral pleura; the parietal pleura lines the thoracic wall.

1.7 Medical Imaging

  • A. CT reconstructs cross-sectional information from multiple X-ray projections.
  • B. PET.

Chapter 2 answer key

2.1 Elements and Atoms: The Building Blocks of Matter

  • A. Eight, because 14 − 6 = 8.
  • B. It lost one electron; its proton number stayed the same.

2.2 Chemical Bonds

  • A. Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen, creating partial charges.
  • B. Electrostatic attraction between opposite charges.

2.3 Chemical Reactions

  • A. It decreases, allowing the reaction to proceed faster.
  • B. Bond breaking requires energy; the complete hydrolysis reaction releases energy overall as products form and stabilize.

2.4 Inorganic Compounds Essential to Human Functioning

  • A. One hundred times greater.
  • B. Water; the dissolved salt contributes solute ions.

2.5 Organic Compounds Essential to Human Functioning

  • A. It can change folding or the active site, affecting substrate binding and catalysis.
  • B. Its hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails support bilayer formation in water.

Chapter 3 answer key

3.1 The Cell Membrane

  • A. No. Facilitated diffusion follows the gradient; movement against it requires another mechanism.
  • B. Water enters because the external concentration of effectively nonpenetrating solute is lower.

3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles

  • A. The Golgi apparatus.
  • B. Microvilli increase membrane surface area for absorption.

3.3 The Nucleus and DNA Replication

  • A. One parental strand in each new DNA double helix.
  • B. TGCA, with the two strands oriented antiparallel.

3.4 Protein Synthesis

  • A. It matches its anticodon to an mRNA codon and delivers the corresponding amino acid.
  • B. Transcription occurs in the nucleus; translation occurs on ribosomes in the cytoplasm or on rough ER.

3.5 Cell Growth and Division

  • A. S phase of interphase.
  • B. Sister chromatids, which move toward opposite poles.

3.6 Cellular Differentiation

  • A. They express different combinations of genes and consequently produce different proteins and structures.
  • B. Division that preserves a stem-cell population by producing at least one stem-cell descendant.

Chapter 4 answer key

4.1 Types of Tissues

  • A. The synovial membrane, a connective tissue membrane.
  • B. Mucous membranes line passages opening to the exterior; serous membranes line closed ventral cavities and cover their organs.

4.2 Epithelial Tissue

  • A. Its thin barrier supports rapid diffusion.
  • B. Every cell contacts the basement membrane, although not all reach the free surface.

4.3 Connective Tissue Supports and Protects

  • A. Parallel collagen bundles resist strong tension along a main axis.
  • B. Fibrocartilage, particularly in the annulus fibrosus.

4.4 Muscle Tissue and Motion

  • A. Cardiac muscle.
  • B. Smooth cells are spindle-shaped, nonstriated, and have one central nucleus; skeletal fibers are cylindrical, striated, and multinucleated.

4.5 Nervous Tissue Mediates Perception and Response

  • A. The axon and its terminals.
  • B. Glia support, regulate, protect, and insulate parts of the signaling system.

4.6 Tissue Injury and Aging

  • A. Fibrosis can fill the defect with scar that lacks the original tissue’s specialized function.
  • B. Yes. Mechanical, thermal, or other sterile damage can trigger it.

Chapter 5 answer key

5.1 Layers of the Skin

  • A. The stratum basale.
  • B. The epidermis has no blood vessels; deeper dermal injury can bleed.

5.2 Accessory Structures of the Skin

  • A. Eccrine sweat glands.
  • B. The shaft consists of dead keratinized cells; the growing matrix is deeper in the follicle.

5.3 Functions of the Integumentary System

  • A. It carries more heat toward the surface, supporting heat transfer to the environment.
  • B. No. It initiates the pathway; liver and kidney processing are also involved.

5.4 Diseases, Disorders, and Injuries of the Integumentary System

  • A. Damage to the barrier increases fluid loss and disrupts normal protection.
  • B. Melanoma.

Chapter 6 answer key

6.1 The Functions of the Skeletal System

  • A. In red bone marrow.
  • B. Muscle generates pulling force on bones that act as levers around a joint.

6.2 Bone Classification

  • A. As a long bone because of its proportions and organization.
  • B. It develops within a tendon; the patella is the standard example.

6.3 Bone Structure

  • A. An osteoclast.
  • B. They allow communication and exchange between osteocytes and routes toward the blood supply.

6.4 Bone Formation and Development

  • A. The epiphyseal growth plate.
  • B. Yes. Remodeling continues even after normal lengthening has ended.

6.5 Fractures: Bone Repair

  • A. Soft-callus formation.
  • B. The bone is broken into multiple fragments.

6.6 Exercise, Nutrition, Hormones, and Bone Tissue

  • A. Reduced mechanical loading shifts remodeling toward loss of bone mass.
  • B. Vitamin C.

6.7 Calcium Homeostasis: Interactions of the Skeletal System and Other Organ Systems

  • A. It increases calcium absorption from the intestine.
  • B. It decreases through negative feedback.

Chapter 7 answer key

7.1 Divisions of the Skeletal System

  • A. Axial.
  • B. They are part of the pectoral girdles that attach and position the upper limbs.

7.2 The Skull

  • A. The perpendicular plate of the ethmoid and the vomer.
  • B. The occipital bone.

7.3 The Vertebral Column

  • A. The axis, C2.
  • B. Cervical and lumbar curves, which develop after birth.

7.4 The Thoracic Cage

  • A. Ribs 11 and 12.
  • B. The sternal angle.

7.5 Embryonic Development of the Axial Skeleton

  • A. They permit molding during birth and expansion as the brain and skull grow.
  • B. The sclerotome.

Chapter 8 answer key

8.1 The Pectoral Girdle

  • A. The clavicle.
  • B. The glenoid cavity.

8.2 Bones of the Upper Limb

  • A. The capitulum.
  • B. The trapezium.

8.3 The Pelvic Girdle and Pelvis

  • A. Ilium, ischium, and pubis.
  • B. Transferring load between the axial skeleton and pelvic girdle/lower limbs.

8.4 Bones of the Lower Limb

  • A. The calcaneus.
  • B. The talus.

8.5 Development of the Appendicular Skeleton

  • A. Programmed cell death in the tissue between developing digits.
  • B. The apical ectodermal ridge.

Chapter 9 answer key

9.1 Classification of Joints

  • A. Synovial and diarthrosis; its ball-and-socket form permits multiaxial movement.
  • B. Amphiarthrosis.

9.2 Fibrous Joints

  • A. The periodontal ligament at a gomphosis.
  • B. An interosseous membrane forming a syndesmosis.

9.3 Cartilaginous Joints

  • A. Hyaline cartilage.
  • B. A cartilaginous symphysis.

9.4 Synovial Joints

  • A. Saddle.
  • B. Usually hyaline surface covering versus a fibrocartilaginous disc.

9.5 Types of Body Movements

  • A. Opposition.
  • B. Circumduction moves the distal end through a cone; rotation turns a bone about its long axis.

9.6 Anatomy of Selected Synovial Joints

  • A. PCL.
  • B. Deeper socket and strong capsule, ligaments, and muscular support suited to weight bearing.

9.7 Development of Joints

  • A. Articular hyaline cartilage.
  • B. Fibrous connective tissue.

Chapter 10 answer key

10.1 Overview of Muscle Tissues

  • A. Excitability.
  • B. Yes, as in isometric contraction.

10.2 Skeletal Muscle

  • A. Perimysium.
  • B. One T-tubule and two terminal cisternae of the SR.

10.3 Muscle Fiber Contraction and Relaxation

  • A. Myosin detaches from actin.
  • B. The A band.

10.4 Nervous System Control of Muscle Tension

  • A. One motor neuron and all fibers it innervates.
  • B. Recruitment activates more units; frequency summation increases force through repeated stimulation before full relaxation.

10.5 Types of Muscle Fibers

  • A. Slow oxidative.
  • B. Fast glycolytic.

10.6 Exercise and Muscle Performance

  • A. Increased mitochondrial content, capillary supply, or myoglobin.
  • B. Fiber size and contractile protein content.

10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue

  • A. Gap junctions.
  • B. No; extracellular calcium also contributes.

10.8 Smooth Muscle

  • A. Calmodulin.
  • B. They coordinate electrical activity and contraction among cells.

10.9 Development and Regeneration of Muscle Tissue

  • A. Fusion of multiple myoblasts.
  • B. Fibrous scar tissue.

Chapter 11 answer key

11.1 Interactions of Skeletal Muscles, Their Fascicle Arrangement, and Their Lever Systems

  • A. Stabilizes an attachment or body part.
  • B. Effort.

11.2 Naming Skeletal Muscles

  • A. Two heads.
  • B. A relationship to the wrist.

11.3 Axial Muscles of the Head, Neck, and Back

  • A. Iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis.
  • B. Abducens, CN VI.

11.4 Axial Muscles of the Abdominal Wall, and Thorax

  • A. It increases.
  • B. Supports pelvic organs and contributes to continence.

11.5 Muscles of the Pectoral Girdle and Upper Limbs

  • A. Supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis.
  • B. Supination.

11.6 Appendicular Muscles of the Pelvic Girdle and Lower Limbs

  • A. Rectus femoris.
  • B. Dorsiflexion and inversion.

Chapter 12 answer key

12.1 Basic Structure and Function of the Nervous System

  • A. Brain and spinal cord.
  • B. A ganglion.

12.2 Nervous Tissue

  • A. Schwann cell.
  • B. Microglia.

12.3 The Function of Nervous Tissue

  • A. Skeletal muscle at a neuromuscular junction.
  • B. A transmitter crosses a gap; an action potential propagates along membrane.

12.4 The Action Potential

  • A. No; firing frequency can increase instead.
  • B. Action potentials regenerate at nodes along a myelinated axon.

12.5 Communication Between Neurons

  • A. Calcium, Ca2+.
  • B. Repeated inputs close in time versus inputs arriving from different locations.
