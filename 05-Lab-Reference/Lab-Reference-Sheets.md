# Lab reference sheets

These lists organize the material from our study plan. Use the instructor's lab list to determine required structures, attachment detail, and identification depth.

## Histology

| Tissue | Recognition clues | Example | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple squamous | One thin layer | Alveoli, endothelium | Exchange or smooth lining |
| Simple cuboidal | Cube-like cells, central nuclei | Kidney tubules | Absorption and secretion |
| Simple columnar | Tall cells, often goblet cells | Intestine | Absorption and secretion |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Nuclei at different heights, cilia | Much of trachea | Mucus transport |
| Stratified squamous keratinized | Many layers, keratinized surface | Epidermis | Protection and reduced water loss |
| Stratified squamous nonkeratinized | Many layers, moist surface | Esophagus | Abrasion resistance |
| Transitional | Variable surface shape | Urinary bladder | Stretch |
| Dense regular connective | Parallel collagen | Tendon | Directional tensile strength |
| Dense irregular connective | Collagen in varied directions | Reticular dermis | Multidirectional strength |
| Hyaline cartilage | Lacunae, glassy matrix | Articular cartilage | Support and low-friction surface |
| Elastic cartilage | Lacunae and elastic fibers | External ear | Flexible support |
| Fibrocartilage | Thick collagen and chondrocytes | Intervertebral disc | Load resistance |

Add other assigned tissues. Practice on unfamiliar images so that recognition depends on tissue features rather than one memorized photograph.

## Tonicity

| External solution | Net water tendency | Typical animal-cell response |
|---|---|---|
| Hypotonic | Into cell | Swelling |
| Isotonic | No sustained net volume change | Approximately stable |
| Hypertonic | Out of cell | Shrinking |

These predictions depend on effectively nonpenetrating solutes. Osmolarity and tonicity are not always interchangeable. Water molecules continue moving in isotonic conditions.
