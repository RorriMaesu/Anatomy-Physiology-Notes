Chapter 8 memory cues

8.1 The Pectoral Girdle

Spine is behind; Socket is lateral. Acromion articulates with the clavicle; coracoid is a projecting attachment site.

Precision check: The scapula’s movement against the thoracic wall is not a direct bone-to-bone synovial joint with the ribs.

8.2 Bones of the Upper Limb

“Some Little Tigers Play; These Tigers Catch Hares” = Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform; Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate. Each row runs lateral to medial.

Precision check: The pisiform lies on the palmar surface of the triquetrum. The radius crosses the ulna during pronation.

8.3 The Pelvic Girdle and Pelvis

“I Sit on Pubic support” locates Ilium superiorly, Ischium posteroinferiorly, Pubis anteroinferiorly. Sitting pressure is borne largely on ischial tuberosities.

Precision check: The acetabulum receives the femoral head; the obturator foramen is not the hip socket.

8.4 Bones of the Lower Limb

“Tiger Cubs Need MILC” = Talus, Calcaneus, Navicular, Medial cuneiform, Intermediate cuneiform, Lateral cuneiform, Cuboid. This is a LIST cue, not a spatial route.

Precision check: The fibula does not articulate with the femur. The ankle’s lateral malleolus is fibular and its medial malleolus is tibial.

8.5 Development of the Appendicular Skeleton

“Bud, Paddle, Digits, Bone” recalls the broad visual progression. Upper limbs rotate outward; lower limbs rotate inward during the relevant developmental stage.

Precision check: Not every skeletal element ossifies at the same time. The developmental sequence is more useful than one universal age.
