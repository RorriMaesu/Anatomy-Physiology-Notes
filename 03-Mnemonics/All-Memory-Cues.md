# Mnemonics and memory cues

Every cue needs its exact expansion and any direction or exception. Chapters 13–28 contain planning prompts rather than detailed study notes.

## Chapter 1 memory cues

## 1.1 Overview of Anatomy and Physiology

Anatomy = arrangement; physiology = performance. The cue distinguishes the questions asked, but structure and function must be studied together.

**Precision check:** A label is not an explanation. “The heart has valves” becomes useful when you explain how valves limit backward flow.

## 1.2 Structural Organization of the Human Body

Chemicals Create Tissues; Organs Organize Organisms = Chemical, Cellular, Tissue, Organ, Organ system, Organism. Use the exact terms when recalling the ladder.

**Precision check:** A tissue is not simply “many identical cells”; extracellular material can be a major component, especially in connective tissue.

## 1.3 Functions of Human Life

A builds Above; C Cuts: Anabolism builds and Catabolism breaks down. “Organized Metabolism Responds, Moves, Develops, Reproduces” recalls the broader life-function headings.

**Precision check:** Growth and differentiation are different. A cell can specialize without simply becoming larger.

## 1.4 Requirements for Human Life

Oxygen Nourishes Temperature-sensitive People = Oxygen, Nutrients, Temperature, Pressure. The phrase recalls categories; nutrients do not literally control every temperature change.

**Precision check:** Oxygen supplies neither calories nor the carbon skeletons of food. Its role in aerobic respiration differs from the roles of nutrients.

## 1.5 Homeostasis

RCE = Receptor Calls Effector, with the Control center interpreting the call. Negative Negates the deviation; Positive Promotes the process.

**Precision check:** Negative and positive describe feedback direction, not whether the outcome is good or bad. The regulated variable must be named.

## 1.6 Anatomical Terminology

Visceral Visits the organ; Parietal is the Partition wall. Proximal = proximity to attachment; Distal = distant from attachment.

**Precision check:** Proximal/distal follow attachment along a limb; they are not interchangeable with superior/inferior. Pleural and pericardial cavities are thin potential spaces.

## 1.7 Medical Imaging

CT = Computed slices; MRI = Magnet; Ultrasound = echoes; PET = tracer activity. These are recall hooks, not full technical definitions.

**Precision check:** MRI does not use X-rays. Ultrasound also avoids ionizing radiation, but imaging suitability depends on the body part and question.

## Chapter 2 memory cues

## 2.1 Elements and Atoms: The Building Blocks of Matter

P is Positive; N is Neutral. “Mass = Massive nuclear particles” recalls protons plus neutrons. CHON names the major body elements.

**Precision check:** Do not confuse mass number of one isotope with an element’s average atomic mass. Changing protons changes the element.

## 2.2 Chemical Bonds

Covalent = Co-own electrons. ANion is Negative. Hydrogen bonds Hold neighbors together.

**Precision check:** The O–H bonds within one water molecule are covalent. A hydrogen bond between water molecules is a different interaction.

## 2.3 Chemical Reactions

Synthesis Stacks; Decomposition Divides; Exchange Exchanges. Enzymes Ease the energy barrier.

**Precision check:** An enzyme changes the rate, not the reaction’s overall energy difference or its equilibrium position.

## 2.4 Inorganic Compounds Essential to Human Functioning

Hydrolysis = Hydro helps split. “Lower pH, more H” recalls direction. One pH step is tenfold.

**Precision check:** A buffer limits change; it does not make every solution neutral or prevent all possible pH changes.

## 2.5 Organic Compounds Essential to Human Functioning

Carbs fuel, Lipids store and line, Proteins perform, Nucleic acids encode. “Sequence → Shape → Service” connects protein levels to function.

**Precision check:** Lipids are not all polymers of a single repeating monomer. A steroid has fused rings, whereas a triglyceride has glycerol plus three fatty acids.

## Chapter 3 memory cues

## 3.1 The Cell Membrane

Hypo = Hippo cell; Hyper = shriveled cell. “3 sodium Out, 2 potassium In” is the pump ratio.

**Precision check:** At equilibrium molecules still move. Isotonic does not mean water stops moving. Osmolarity and tonicity are not always identical.

## 3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles

Rough ER = Ribosomes; Golgi = packaging and shipping; Lysosomes = digestion; Peroxisomes = peroxide processing. These analogies support, but do not replace, the specific functions.

**Precision check:** Ribosomes are not membrane-bound. Mitochondria are not the only site where cells generate ATP.

## 3.3 The Nucleus and DNA Replication

AT the Car Garage = A–T and C–G. Helicase unHelixes; Polymerase Produces; Ligase Links.

**Precision check:** DNA copying does not double the chromosome count immediately. It produces sister chromatids attached at a centromere.

## 3.4 Protein Synthesis

Transcription = Transcribe into RNA; Translation = Translate into protein. “Introns Interrupt; Exons Express” recalls splicing, although some retained exons are untranslated.

**Precision check:** Read a genetic code table using the mRNA codon, not the DNA template or tRNA anticodon.

## 3.5 Cell Growth and Division

PMAT = Please Make Another Two: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase. Metaphase = Middle; Anaphase = Apart.

**Precision check:** Interphase is active growth and preparation, not a universal resting period. Mitosis and the whole cell cycle are not synonyms.

## 3.6 Cellular Differentiation

T → P → M = Total potential, Plenty of body types, Multiple related types. Use the formal definitions rather than treating the cue as a precise potency test.

**Precision check:** Differentiation usually does not mean the cell discards every gene it no longer expresses.

## Chapter 4 memory cues

## 4.1 Types of Tissues

ECMN = Every Cell Makes Networks: Epithelial, Connective, Muscle, Nervous. “Ecto exterior and electrical; Meso movement; Endo inner lining” is only a broad developmental cue.

**Precision check:** An organ contains several tissue types; identifying one tissue in a slide does not classify the whole organ as that tissue.

## 4.2 Epithelial Tissue

Simple = Single layer; Stratified = Stacked. “Mero mails, Apo pinches, Holo whole cell” recalls secretion mechanisms.

**Precision check:** Classify stratified epithelium by the apical cells, not the deepest cells. Avascular does not mean the tissue has no nerves.

## 4.3 Connective Tissue Supports and Protects

Collagen Cables resist pulls; Elastic Expands and recoils; Reticular Retains a network. Cartilage: Hyaline = support, Elastic = bend, Fibro = force.

**Precision check:** Blood is connective tissue despite its fluid appearance. Cartilage nutrition depends on diffusion, helping explain limited repair.

## 4.4 Muscle Tissue and Motion

Skeletal = Stripes and Several nuclei; Cardiac = Connected branches; Smooth = Spindle and Single nucleus.

**Precision check:** Striated does not mean voluntary: cardiac muscle is striated and involuntary. “Smooth” describes microscopic appearance.

## 4.5 Nervous Tissue Mediates Perception and Response

Dendrites Detect; Axons Announce. This captures the typical information route but does not describe every specialized neuron.

**Precision check:** A nerve is a bundle of axons in the peripheral nervous system; it is not one whole neuron.

## 4.6 Tissue Injury and Aging

Repair often follows “Close, Clean, Construct, Consolidate”: clot, inflammatory cleanup, new tissue, remodeling. It is a memory model, not a rigid timing rule.

**Precision check:** Inflammation is not synonymous with infection. Sterile injury can cause inflammation.

## Chapter 5 memory cues

## 5.1 Layers of the Skin

“Come Let’s Get Sun Burned” = Corneum, Lucidum, Granulosum, Spinosum, Basale, SUPERFICIAL to DEEP. Omit Lucidum for thin skin.

**Precision check:** The reticular dermis is dense irregular connective tissue. The hypodermis is not an epidermal layer.

## 5.2 Accessory Structures of the Skin

Eccrine Exits onto Epidermis; Sebaceous supplies Sebum. Hair “Grows, Changes, Rests” recalls anagen, catagen, telogen.

**Precision check:** Do not infer a gland’s secretion mechanism solely from its name. Human apocrine sweat glands principally use exocytotic secretion despite the historical name.

## 5.3 Functions of the Integumentary System

PSTV = Protect, Sense, Thermoregulate, Vitamin D. “Dilate to Dissipate” recalls heat loss through increased skin blood flow.

**Precision check:** Sweat must evaporate to provide effective cooling. Sweat production alone is not the same as heat removal.

## 5.4 Diseases, Disorders, and Injuries of the Integumentary System

ABCDE recalls melanoma recognition features: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter, Evolving. It is a screening memory cue, not a diagnosis or a rule excluding small melanomas.

**Precision check:** A deeper burn may damage sensory endings and reduce sensation locally. Pain intensity alone does not reliably identify depth.

## Chapter 6 memory cues

## 6.1 The Functions of the Skeletal System

“Support Protects Movers; Minerals, Blood, Fat” recalls support, protection, movement, mineral storage, hematopoiesis, and fat storage.

**Precision check:** Bone is living, vascular tissue. A skeleton is not merely a static framework of mineral.

## 6.2 Bone Classification

“Long Shorts Fit Irregular Shapes” = Long, Short, Flat, Irregular, Sesamoid. A tiny phalanx is still a long bone.

**Precision check:** A flat bone can be curved. “Long bone” does not mean every physically large bone belongs in that category.

## 6.3 Bone Structure

BLASTs Build; CLASTs Clear; CYTEs Care. Lacunae are little rooms; canaliculi connect them.

**Precision check:** Hydroxyapatite is inorganic mineral. Spongy bone is mineralized tissue with spaces, not soft unmineralized bone.

## 6.4 Bone Formation and Development

“Resting People Mature and Calcify” = Reserve, Proliferation, Maturation/hypertrophy, Calcification. “Intra in membrane; Endo replaces cartilage.”

**Precision check:** Cartilage does not simply turn into bone cell-for-cell. The cartilage model is replaced by newly formed osseous tissue.

## 6.5 Fractures: Bone Repair

“Hematoma Softens; Bone Remodels” = Hematoma, Soft callus, Bony callus, Remodeling.

**Precision check:** A soft callus does not have the mechanical strength of mature remodeled bone. Open reduction refers to a surgical approach, not necessarily a fracture through skin.

## 6.6 Exercise, Nutrition, Hormones, and Bone Tissue

Vitamin C builds Collagen; Vitamin D supports Digestive calcium uptake. “Load lays bone” recalls the response to appropriate mechanical demand.

**Precision check:** The study goal is understanding nutrient and hormone functions, not memorizing supplement doses. Bone quantity and mineralization quality are different variables.

## 6.7 Calcium Homeostasis: Interactions of the Skeletal System and Other Organ Systems

PTH = Puts calcium Higher. CalciTONin can Tone down blood calcium. Always attach the cue to a target organ and mechanism.

**Precision check:** PTH’s stimulation of osteoclast-mediated resorption is indirect. Blood calcium concentration is not a direct measure of total bone strength.

## Chapter 7 memory cues

## 7.1 Divisions of the Skeletal System

Axial = Axis; Appendicular = Appendages and attachments. Remember 80 + 126 = 206 as the standard adult count.

**Precision check:** A girdle belongs to the appendicular skeleton even though it lies close to the trunk. Bone counts vary with age and individual anatomy.

## 7.2 The Skull

“Old People From Texas Eat Spiders” = Occipital, Parietal, Frontal, Temporal, Ethmoid, Sphenoid. These are SIX TYPES; paired P and T give EIGHT bones.

**Precision check:** Superior and middle nasal conchae are parts of the ethmoid; the inferior nasal conchae are separate bones.

## 7.3 The Vertebral Column

Breakfast at 7, lunch at 12, dinner at 5 = C7, T12, L5. Atlas carries the head; Axis supplies the rotation pivot.

**Precision check:** The vertebral foramen carries the spinal cord region; intervertebral foramina provide routes for spinal nerves. Do not interchange them.

## 7.4 The Thoracic Cage

7 true, 3 indirect, 2 floating = 12 pairs. “Manubrium Begins, Xiphoid Ends” helps orient the sternum.

**Precision check:** Floating ribs are a subset of false ribs, not an additional group beyond the twelve pairs.

## 7.5 Embryonic Development of the Axial Skeleton

Vault forms in a membrane; Base begins with cartilage. “Fontanelles Flex for birth and growth.”

**Precision check:** Do not memorize a single closure age as an exact deadline for every person. Different sutures and fontanelles have different developmental schedules.

## Chapter 8 memory cues

## 8.1 The Pectoral Girdle

Spine is behind; Socket is lateral. Acromion articulates with the clavicle; coracoid is a projecting attachment site.

**Precision check:** The scapula’s movement against the thoracic wall is not a direct bone-to-bone synovial joint with the ribs.

## 8.2 Bones of the Upper Limb

“Some Little Tigers Play; These Tigers Catch Hares” = Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform; Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate. Each row runs lateral to medial.

**Precision check:** The pisiform lies on the palmar surface of the triquetrum. The radius crosses the ulna during pronation.

## 8.3 The Pelvic Girdle and Pelvis

“I Sit on Pubic support” locates Ilium superiorly, Ischium posteroinferiorly, Pubis anteroinferiorly. Sitting pressure is borne largely on ischial tuberosities.

**Precision check:** The acetabulum receives the femoral head; the obturator foramen is not the hip socket.

## 8.4 Bones of the Lower Limb

“Tiger Cubs Need MILC” = Talus, Calcaneus, Navicular, Medial cuneiform, Intermediate cuneiform, Lateral cuneiform, Cuboid. This is a LIST cue, not a spatial route.

**Precision check:** The fibula does not articulate with the femur. The ankle’s lateral malleolus is fibular and its medial malleolus is tibial.

## 8.5 Development of the Appendicular Skeleton

“Bud, Paddle, Digits, Bone” recalls the broad visual progression. Upper limbs rotate outward; lower limbs rotate inward during the relevant developmental stage.

**Precision check:** Not every skeletal element ossifies at the same time. The developmental sequence is more useful than one universal age.

## Chapter 9 memory cues

## 9.1 Classification of Joints

Still, a little, dynamic = synarthrosis, amphiarthrosis, diarthrosis.

**Precision check:** Structural and functional labels answer different questions.

## 9.2 Fibrous Joints

Seams, sockets, straps = sutures, gomphoses, syndesmoses.

**Precision check:** Not every fibrous joint permits the same amount of movement.

## 9.3 Cartilaginous Joints

Symphysis supplies a fibrous cushion.

**Precision check:** Vertebral bodies form symphyses; facet articulations are synovial joints.

## 9.4 Synovial Joints

Please Help People Carry Shopping Bags = Plane, Hinge, Pivot, Condyloid, Saddle, Ball-and-socket.

**Precision check:** Articular cartilage is usually hyaline; a meniscus is fibrocartilage. Accessories are not present in every joint.

## 9.5 Types of Body Movements

ABduct away; ADduct add back; supination holds soup.

**Precision check:** Pronation and supination occur at radioulnar joints, not the elbow hinge alone.

## 9.6 Anatomy of Selected Synovial Joints

ACL checks anterior tibia; PCL checks posterior tibia. Atlas-occiput nods yes; atlas-axis turns no.

**Precision check:** Switching from tibial to femoral movement reverses the corresponding displacement description.

## 9.7 Development of Joints

Cartilage caps; capsule contains.

**Precision check:** Do not remove all cartilage from a mature synovial-joint drawing.

## Chapter 10 memory cues

## 10.1 Overview of Muscle Tissues

Excite, contract, extend, recoil.

**Precision check:** Contraction does not always mean visible shortening.

## 10.2 Skeletal Muscle

Epi entire muscle; peri packet or fascicle; endo each fiber.

**Precision check:** A muscle fiber is a cell; a myofibril is a structure within it.

## 10.3 Muscle Fiber Contraction and Relaxation

Calcium uncovers; ATP detaches and resets.

**Precision check:** Actin and myosin slide; they do not become shorter filaments.

## 10.4 Nervous System Control of Muscle Tension

Recruitment recruits units; summation stacks responses over time.

**Precision check:** Greater whole-muscle force does not require a taller individual action potential.

## 10.5 Types of Muscle Fibers

Slow sustains; fast glycolytic fires forcefully and fatigues faster.

**Precision check:** A muscle is not necessarily made of only one fiber type.

## 10.6 Exercise and Muscle Performance

Hypertrophy bigger cells; hyperplasia more cells.

**Precision check:** Delayed soreness is not simply retained lactic acid.

## 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue

Desmosomes hold; gap junctions communicate.

**Precision check:** Each heartbeat does not require a somatic motor-neuron command.

## 10.8 Smooth Muscle

Smooth uses CaM: calcium-calmodulin, then kinase.

**Precision check:** Do not put troponin into the smooth-muscle regulatory pathway.

## 10.9 Development and Regeneration of Muscle Tissue

Myoblasts merge; satellite cells support repair.

**Precision check:** The three muscle types do not have equal repair capacity.

## Chapter 12 memory cues

**Precision check:** Somatic activity includes reflexes as well as conscious movement.

## 12.2 Nervous Tissue

Oligodendrocytes myelinate CNS axons; Schwann cells myelinate peripheral axons.

**Precision check:** One Schwann cell forms one myelin segment; one oligodendrocyte can myelinate segments on multiple axons.

## 12.3 The Function of Nervous Tissue

**Precision check:** Not every motor response waits for conscious perception.

## 12.4 The Action Potential

**Precision check:** The Na+/K+ pump maintains gradients over time; it does not directly cause rapid repolarization.

## 12.5 Communication Between Neurons

Temporal time; spatial separate places. Calcium triggers release; receptors determine response.

**Precision check:** A transmitter's effect depends on its receptor and target cell.
