Chapter 3 memory cues

3.1 The Cell Membrane

Hypo = Hippo cell; Hyper = shriveled cell. “3 sodium Out, 2 potassium In” is the pump ratio.

Precision check: At equilibrium molecules still move. Isotonic does not mean water stops moving. Osmolarity and tonicity are not always identical.

3.2 The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles

Rough ER = Ribosomes; Golgi = packaging and shipping; Lysosomes = digestion; Peroxisomes = peroxide processing. These analogies support, but do not replace, the specific functions.

Precision check: Ribosomes are not membrane-bound. Mitochondria are not the only site where cells generate ATP.

3.3 The Nucleus and DNA Replication

AT the Car Garage = A–T and C–G. Helicase unHelixes; Polymerase Produces; Ligase Links.

Precision check: DNA copying does not double the chromosome count immediately. It produces sister chromatids attached at a centromere.

3.4 Protein Synthesis

Transcription = Transcribe into RNA; Translation = Translate into protein. “Introns Interrupt; Exons Express” recalls splicing, although some retained exons are untranslated.

Precision check: Read a genetic code table using the mRNA codon, not the DNA template or tRNA anticodon.

3.5 Cell Growth and Division

PMAT = Please Make Another Two: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase. Metaphase = Middle; Anaphase = Apart.

Precision check: Interphase is active growth and preparation, not a universal resting period. Mitosis and the whole cell cycle are not synonyms.

3.6 Cellular Differentiation

T → P → M = Total potential, Plenty of body types, Multiple related types. Use the formal definitions rather than treating the cue as a precise potency test.

Precision check: Differentiation usually does not mean the cell discards every gene it no longer expresses.
