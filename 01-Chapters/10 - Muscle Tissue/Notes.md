## 10.2 Skeletal Muscle

[OpenStax section 10.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-2-skeletal-muscle)

- Organization proceeds from muscle to fascicle, fiber, myofibril, sarcomere, and myofilaments. Epimysium surrounds the whole muscle; perimysium surrounds fascicles; endomysium surrounds individual fibers. Learn sarcolemma, sarcoplasm, sarcoplasmic reticulum, T-tubules, triads, tendons, and the neuromuscular junction.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw nested organization, a labeled sarcomere, and an NMJ. Trace force through connective tissue to a tendon.

**Memory cue:** Epi entire muscle; peri packet or fascicle; endo each fiber.

**Precision check:** A muscle fiber is a cell; a myofibril is a structure within it.

## 10.3 Muscle Fiber Contraction and Relaxation

[OpenStax section 10.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-3-muscle-fiber-contraction-and-relaxation)

- Follow motor-neuron signal, acetylcholine release, end-plate depolarization, muscle action potential, T-tubule signaling, SR calcium release, troponin binding, tropomyosin shift, and cross-bridge cycling. ATP binding detaches myosin; ATP hydrolysis repositions the head. Relaxation involves ending ACh signaling and pumping calcium into SR. Compare creatine phosphate, glycolysis, and aerobic ATP production. During shortening, Z discs approach; I band and H zone narrow; A band and filament lengths stay constant.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw excitation-contraction coupling, the cross-bridge cycle, and a relaxed/shortened sarcomere. Make an ATP-source comparison.

**Memory cue:** Calcium uncovers; ATP detaches and resets.

**Precision check:** Actin and myosin slide; they do not become shorter filaments.

## 10.4 Nervous System Control of Muscle Tension

[OpenStax section 10.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-4-nervous-system-control-of-muscle-tension)

- A motor unit contains one motor neuron and all muscle fibers it innervates. Learn twitch latent, contraction, and relaxation phases; recruitment; frequency summation; incomplete and complete tetanus; treppe; tone; and length-tension relationships. Excessive shortening or stretching reduces effective force generation.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw twitch and summation traces. Compare more active motor units with more frequent stimulation.

**Memory cue:** Recruitment recruits units; summation stacks responses over time.

**Precision check:** Greater whole-muscle force does not require a taller individual action potential.

- Compare slow oxidative, fast oxidative, and fast glycolytic fibers by speed, fatigue resistance, myoglobin, mitochondria, capillaries, and ATP sources. Most skeletal muscles contain mixtures, with proportions and recruitment suited to function.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Make a three-row fiber matrix and connect each pattern to an activity.

## 10.7 Cardiac Muscle Tissue

[OpenStax section 10.7](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-7-cardiac-muscle-tissue)

- Cardiac muscle cells are striated and branched with usually one central nucleus. Intercalated discs contain mechanical junctions and gap junctions. Pacemaker activity helps coordinate contraction. Calcium from extracellular fluid and SR participates in contraction.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw intercalated-disc connections and compare skeletal with cardiac activation.

**Memory cue:** Desmosomes hold; gap junctions communicate.

**Precision check:** Each heartbeat does not require a somatic motor-neuron command.

## 10.8 Smooth Muscle

[OpenStax section 10.8](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-8-smooth-muscle)

- Smooth muscle cells are spindle-shaped and nonstriated, with dense bodies rather than sarcomeres. Calcium binds calmodulin; the complex activates myosin light-chain kinase; myosin phosphorylation supports cross-bridge activity. Compare single-unit and multiunit organization, neural/hormonal/local stimuli, and economical sustained tone.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Compare skeletal and smooth regulation in parallel pathways. Label calmodulin and kinase rather than troponin.

**Memory cue:** Smooth uses CaM: calcium-calmodulin, then kinase.

**Precision check:** Do not put troponin into the smooth-muscle regulatory pathway.

## 10.9 Development and Regeneration of Muscle Tissue

[OpenStax section 10.9](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/10-9-development-and-regeneration-of-muscle-tissue)

- Muscle develops largely from mesoderm. Myoblast fusion forms multinucleated skeletal fibers; satellite cells contribute limited repair. Smooth muscle generally has greater regenerative capacity than cardiac muscle. Extensive cardiac damage is often replaced by fibrous scar.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Make a development and repair comparison for skeletal, cardiac, and smooth tissue.

**Memory cue:** Myoblasts merge; satellite cells support repair.

**Precision check:** The three muscle types do not have equal repair capacity.
