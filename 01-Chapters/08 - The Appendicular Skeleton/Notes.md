# Chapter 8 The Appendicular Skeleton

## 8.1 The Pectoral Girdle

[OpenStax section 8.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-1-the-pectoral-girdle)

- Each pectoral girdle contains a clavicle and scapula. The sternoclavicular joint is the upper limb’s direct bony connection to the axial skeleton.
- The clavicle acts as a strut positioning the shoulder. Distinguish its medial sternal end from lateral acromial end and identify major ligament attachment markings.
- The scapula has spine, acromion, coracoid process, glenoid cavity, supraspinous and infraspinous fossae, subscapular fossa, borders, and angles.
- The shallow glenoid cavity supports mobility; muscular and ligamentous support contribute stability. Scapular movement expands the upper limb’s range.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw anterior and posterior scapular views and both clavicular ends. Practice siding a scapula: glenoid lateral, spine posterior, inferior angle down. List the articulating partners.

**Memory cue:** Spine is behind; Socket is lateral. Acromion articulates with the clavicle; coracoid is a projecting attachment site.

**Precision check:** The scapula’s movement against the thoracic wall is not a direct bone-to-bone synovial joint with the ribs.

## 8.2 Bones of the Upper Limb

[OpenStax section 8.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-2-bones-of-the-upper-limb)

- The upper limb contains humerus, radius, ulna, eight carpals, five metacarpals, and fourteen phalanges on each side. Radius is lateral, on the thumb side, in anatomical position.
- Humeral landmarks include head, anatomical and surgical necks, tubercles, deltoid tuberosity, capitulum, trochlea, epicondyles, and fossae. The capitulum meets radius; the trochlea meets ulna.
- Ulna landmarks include olecranon, coronoid process, trochlear notch, radial notch, head, and styloid. Radius landmarks include head, neck, radial tuberosity, ulnar notch, and styloid.
- The thumb has two phalanges; other fingers have three. Number metacarpals and digits from thumb I to little finger V. Learn carpal rows and the boundaries of the carpal tunnel.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw anterior/posterior humerus, both forearm bones, and a palmar hand. Practice siding bones and naming their joint partners. Mark carpals in rows from thumb to little finger.

**Memory cue:** “Some Little Tigers Play; These Tigers Catch Hares” = Scaphoid, Lunate, Triquetrum, Pisiform; Trapezium, Trapezoid, Capitate, Hamate. Each row runs lateral to medial.

**Precision check:** The pisiform lies on the palmar surface of the triquetrum. The radius crosses the ulna during pronation.

## 8.3 The Pelvic Girdle and Pelvis

[OpenStax section 8.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-3-the-pelvic-girdle-and-pelvis)

- Each adult hip bone consists of fused ilium, ischium, and pubis. All three contribute to the acetabulum. The pelvic girdle comprises the two hip bones; the bony pelvis also includes sacrum and coccyx.
- Learn iliac crest and spines, iliac fossa, auricular surface, greater sciatic notch, ischial spine and tuberosity, pubic rami and symphyseal surface, acetabulum, and obturator foramen.
- The sacroiliac joints transfer weight from axial skeleton to lower limbs; the pubic symphysis connects the anterior hip bones.
- Compare pelvic inlet, outlet, true and false pelvis, and the textbook’s typical sex-associated pelvic patterns. Population patterns have overlap and do not determine an individual’s gender.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw one hip bone in medial and lateral views and the complete pelvis. Color the three developmental components. Compare load-bearing features with the pectoral girdle.

**Memory cue:** “I Sit on Pubic support” locates Ilium superiorly, Ischium posteroinferiorly, Pubis anteroinferiorly. Sitting pressure is borne largely on ischial tuberosities.

**Precision check:** The acetabulum receives the femoral head; the obturator foramen is not the hip socket.

## 8.4 Bones of the Lower Limb

[OpenStax section 8.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-4-bones-of-the-lower-limb)

- Each lower limb contains femur, patella, tibia, fibula, seven tarsals, five metatarsals, and fourteen phalanges. Tibia is the main weight-bearing leg bone; fibula lies laterally.
- Learn femoral head, neck, trochanters, linea aspera, condyles, and epicondyles; tibial condyles, tuberosity, anterior border, and medial malleolus; fibular head and lateral malleolus.
- Tarsals are talus, calcaneus, navicular, cuboid, and medial, intermediate, and lateral cuneiforms. Talus receives load from the leg; calcaneus forms the heel.
- The great toe has two phalanges. Medial and lateral longitudinal arches and the transverse arch distribute load and adapt to surfaces.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw femur, tibia/fibula, and superior/inferior foot views. Number digits from great toe I. Trace the route of body weight through tibia, talus, and foot.

**Memory cue:** “Tiger Cubs Need MILC” = Talus, Calcaneus, Navicular, Medial cuneiform, Intermediate cuneiform, Lateral cuneiform, Cuboid. This is a LIST cue, not a spatial route.

**Precision check:** The fibula does not articulate with the femur. The ankle’s lateral malleolus is fibular and its medial malleolus is tibial.

## 8.5 Development of the Appendicular Skeleton

[OpenStax section 8.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-5-development-of-the-appendicular-skeleton)

- Limb buds appear early in embryonic development. The apical ectodermal ridge supports outgrowth, while patterning signals organize future limb structures.
- Mesenchymal condensations become cartilage models for many limb bones. Primary and secondary ossification centers later replace much of that cartilage with bone.
- Programmed cell death helps separate developing digits. Upper and lower limbs rotate in different directions, contributing to their adult anatomical orientations.
- Most appendicular bones form primarily by endochondral ossification. The clavicle is a major intramembranous exception with additional developmental complexity.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw a limb-bud timeline: bud, paddle, separated digits, cartilage model, ossification, growth plate, fusion. Compare upper-limb lateral rotation with lower-limb medial rotation.

**Memory cue:** “Bud, Paddle, Digits, Bone” recalls the broad visual progression. Upper limbs rotate outward; lower limbs rotate inward during the relevant developmental stage.

**Precision check:** Not every skeletal element ossifies at the same time. The developmental sequence is more useful than one universal age.
