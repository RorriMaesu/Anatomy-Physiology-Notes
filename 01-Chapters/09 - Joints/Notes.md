## 9.1 Classification of Joints

[OpenStax section 9.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-1-classification-of-joints)

- Structural classification describes what joins bones and whether a cavity exists: fibrous, cartilaginous, or synovial. Functional classification describes movement: synarthrosis, amphiarthrosis, or diarthrosis. All synovial joints are diarthroses. Distinguish uniaxial, biaxial, and multiaxial movement.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Give skull sutures, pubic symphysis, shoulder, elbow, and a syndesmosis both structural and functional labels.

**Memory cue:** Still, a little, dynamic = synarthrosis, amphiarthrosis, diarthrosis.

**Precision check:** Structural and functional labels answer different questions.

## 9.2 Fibrous Joints

[OpenStax section 9.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-2-fibrous-joints)

- Fibrous joints connect structures through fibrous connective tissue without a synovial cavity. Sutures join skull bones. Gomphoses anchor teeth through periodontal ligaments. Syndesmoses connect bones using ligaments or interosseous membranes. Their permitted movement varies.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw a suture, gomphosis, and syndesmosis; label the connecting tissue and explain stability versus mobility.

**Memory cue:** Seams, sockets, straps = sutures, gomphoses, syndesmoses.

**Precision check:** Not every fibrous joint permits the same amount of movement.

## 9.3 Cartilaginous Joints

[OpenStax section 9.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-3-cartilaginous-joints)

- Synchondroses use hyaline cartilage, as in a growth plate or first sternocostal joint. Symphyses include fibrocartilage, as in the pubic symphysis and intervertebral discs. A closing growth plate becomes a bony union or synostosis.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Build a cartilage-type, example, and movement table. Draw growth plate, pubic symphysis, and intervertebral disc.

**Memory cue:** Symphysis supplies a fibrous cushion.

**Precision check:** Vertebral bodies form symphyses; facet articulations are synovial joints.

## 9.4 Synovial Joints

[OpenStax section 9.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-4-synovial-joints)

- A synovial joint has articular cartilage, a fluid-containing cavity, and an articular capsule with fibrous and synovial components. Ligaments reinforce it. Bursae and tendon sheaths reduce friction; discs and menisci occur in selected joints. Compare plane, hinge, pivot, condyloid, saddle, and ball-and-socket forms. OpenStax describes plane joints as allowing small constrained multiaxial movements.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw a generic joint. Build a six-shape matrix with example, shape, axes, and permitted movement.

**Memory cue:** Please Help People Carry Shopping Bags = Plane, Hinge, Pivot, Condyloid, Saddle, Ball-and-socket.

**Precision check:** Articular cartilage is usually hyaline; a meniscus is fibrocartilage. Accessories are not present in every joint.

## 9.5 Types of Body Movements

[OpenStax section 9.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-5-types-of-body-movements)

- Learn flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, circumduction, rotation, pronation/supination, dorsiflexion/plantar flexion, inversion/eversion, elevation/depression, protraction/retraction, opposition/reposition, excursion, and scapular rotation. Name the joint and reference axis in each example.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Demonstrate each movement safely and draw its direction on an anatomical outline. Include special reference axes for fingers and toes.

**Memory cue:** ABduct away; ADduct add back; supination holds soup.

**Precision check:** Pronation and supination occur at radioulnar joints, not the elbow hinge alone.

## 9.6 Anatomy of Selected Synovial Joints

[OpenStax section 9.6](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-6-anatomy-of-selected-synovial-joints)

- Study vertebral, temporomandibular, shoulder, elbow, hip, knee, ankle, and foot joints. Compare shallow shoulder and deeper hip sockets. The knee's menisci distribute load. ACL limits anterior tibial displacement relative to femur; PCL limits posterior tibial displacement. Collateral ligaments resist sideways stresses. Talocrural movement is dorsiflexion and plantar flexion.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Make one page per major joint: bones, subtype, movements, stabilizers, and injury mechanism. Draw knee ligaments with tibia and femur identified.

**Memory cue:** ACL checks anterior tibia; PCL checks posterior tibia. Atlas-occiput nods yes; atlas-axis turns no.

**Precision check:** Switching from tibial to femoral movement reverses the corresponding displacement description.

## 9.7 Development of Joints

[OpenStax section 9.7](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/9-7-development-of-joints)

- Developing joints arise in mesenchymal tissue. A joint interzone forms between cartilage models at future synovial joints; a central cavity and peripheral capsule develop. Articular cartilage remains at bone ends. Fibrous connective tissue remains between bones at skull sutures.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw mesenchyme, cartilage models, interzone, and mature joint. Compare tissue retained at sutures and synovial surfaces.

**Memory cue:** Cartilage caps; capsule contains.

**Precision check:** Do not remove all cartilage from a mature synovial-joint drawing.
