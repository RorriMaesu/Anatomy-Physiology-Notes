## 11.1 Interactions of Skeletal Muscles, Their Fascicle Arrangement, and Their Lever Systems

[OpenStax section 11.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-1-interactions-of-skeletal-muscles-their-fascicle-arrangement-and-their-lever-systems)

- Define origin, insertion, prime mover, antagonist, synergist, and fixator. Compare fascicle arrangements: parallel, fusiform, convergent, circular, and pennate. In lever systems identify fulcrum, effort, and load; the middle component identifies lever class.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Analyze one movement by muscle roles. Draw first-, second-, and third-class levers with real examples.

**Memory cue:** Middle component: 1 fulcrum, 2 load, 3 effort.

**Precision check:** Origin is not absolutely immovable in every activity.

## 11.2 Naming Skeletal Muscles

[OpenStax section 11.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-2-naming-skeletal-muscles)

- Muscle names can describe location, shape, size, fiber direction, number of heads, attachments, or action. Decode sternocleidomastoid, rectus abdominis, biceps brachii, and flexor carpi radialis.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Make a word-part table and explain each example aloud. Add the anatomical information the name does not provide.

**Memory cue:** Rectus straight; oblique angled; transversus across; biceps two heads; triceps three.

**Precision check:** A muscle name does not state every action or attachment.

## 11.3 Axial Muscles of the Head, Neck, and Back

[OpenStax section 11.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-3-axial-muscles-of-the-head-neck-and-back)

- Organize facial-expression muscles, extraocular muscles, mastication, tongue movement, suprahyoid/infrahyoid groups, head movement, and intrinsic back muscles. For every assigned muscle record location, origin, insertion, action, and assigned innervation. Keep region sheets separate so identification remains manageable.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw front/side head and neck, eye movements, and posterior trunk. Build muscle records from the section figures.

**Memory cue:** LR6 SO4, remaining extraocular movement muscles 3. I Love Spine = Iliocostalis, Longissimus, Spinalis, lateral to medial.

**Precision check:** Eye-muscle action depends on eye position; do not treat a mnemonic as a complete movement description.

## 11.4 Axial Muscles of the Abdominal Wall, and Thorax

[OpenStax section 11.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-4-axial-muscles-of-the-abdominal-wall-and-thorax)

- Study rectus abdominis, external/internal obliques, transversus abdominis, quadratus lumborum, diaphragm, intercostals, pelvic floor, and perineal muscles. Link fiber direction and attachment to trunk movement, ventilation, organ support, and continence.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw abdominal layers, diaphragm before/after contraction, and pelvic-floor support. Add assigned origin/insertion/action records.

**Memory cue:** External oblique fibers resemble hands-in-pockets direction.

**Precision check:** Quiet expiration usually relies largely on passive recoil, not forceful contraction of all expiratory muscles.

## 11.5 Muscles of the Pectoral Girdle and Upper Limbs

[OpenStax section 11.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-5-muscles-of-the-pectoral-girdle-and-upper-limbs)

- Group muscles by scapular positioning, humeral movement, elbow action, forearm rotation, wrist/digit movement, and intrinsic hand control. Learn SITS and its stabilization function. Distinguish teres major from teres minor. Complete regional muscle records and relate attachment lines to action.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw anterior/posterior upper limb and hand. Build movement-first groups, then origins, insertions, and assigned nerves.

**Memory cue:** SITS = Supraspinatus, Infraspinatus, Teres minor, Subscapularis. PAD/DAB = Palmar interossei ADduct, Dorsal interossei ABduct.

**Precision check:** Teres major is not a rotator-cuff muscle.

## 11.6 Appendicular Muscles of the Pelvic Girdle and Lower Limbs

[OpenStax section 11.6](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-6-appendicular-muscles-of-the-pelvic-girdle-and-lower-limbs)

- Organize hip flexors, extensors, abductors, adductors, and rotators; quadriceps; hamstrings; leg compartments; and foot/toe actions. Quadriceps extend the knee; rectus femoris also flexes the hip. Tibialis anterior dorsiflexes and inverts. Gastrocnemius and soleus plantar-flex.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw anterior/posterior lower limb and a compartment-action map. Create origin/insertion/action records for each assigned muscle.

**Memory cue:** Quadriceps = Rectus femoris and Vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius. Hamstrings = Biceps femoris, Semitendinosus, Semimembranosus.

**Precision check:** The short head of biceps femoris does not cross the hip.
