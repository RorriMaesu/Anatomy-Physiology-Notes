## 12.2 Nervous Tissue

[OpenStax section 12.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue)

- Learn neuron parts and multipolar, bipolar, and pseudounipolar forms. Compare CNS astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, and ependymal cells with PNS Schwann and satellite cells. Myelin and nodes support rapid conduction. Astrocytes regulate the environment; microglia perform immune surveillance; specialized ependymal-related tissue participates in CSF production.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw neurons and myelin; build a six-glial-cell table with location and function.

**Memory cue:** Oligodendrocytes myelinate CNS axons; Schwann cells myelinate peripheral axons.

**Precision check:** One Schwann cell forms one myelin segment; one oligodendrocyte can myelinate segments on multiple axons.

## 12.4 The Action Potential

[OpenStax section 12.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-4-the-action-potential)

- Resting voltage depends on ion gradients and selective permeability. Threshold triggers a regenerative response: voltage-gated Na+ channels open, then inactivate; K+ conductance rises and repolarizes the membrane. Delayed K+ closure contributes to after-hyperpolarization. Learn absolute/relative refractory periods, continuous/saltatory conduction, and effects of myelin and axon diameter.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw voltage versus time, with resting potential, threshold, rise, fall, and undershoot. Add channel states and ion directions. Textbook voltages are representative, not universal.

**Memory cue:** Na+ rises; K+ returns the voltage.

**Precision check:** The Na+/K+ pump maintains gradients over time; it does not directly cause rapid repolarization.

## 12.5 Communication Between Neurons

[OpenStax section 12.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-5-communication-between-neurons)

- Compare graded potentials with action potentials. Learn EPSPs/IPSPs, temporal/spatial summation, chemical/electrical synapses, calcium-triggered release, ionotropic/metabotropic receptors, transmitter clearance, and transmitter families. Group ACh, amino acids, biogenic amines, and neuropeptides with examples.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw a chemical synapse and a summation comparison. Build transmitter-family and graded/action-potential tables.

**Memory cue:** Temporal time; spatial separate places. Calcium triggers release; receptors determine response.

**Precision check:** A transmitter's effect depends on its receptor and target cell.
