## 6.1 The Functions of the Skeletal System

[OpenStax section 6.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-1-the-functions-of-the-skeletal-system)

- Bones support the body and act as levers for skeletal muscles. They protect structures such as brain, spinal cord, thoracic organs, and pelvic organs.
- Bone stores calcium and phosphate and exchanges minerals with body fluids. Red marrow performs hematopoiesis, the production of blood cells.
- Yellow marrow stores triglycerides. The distribution of red and yellow marrow changes with age and anatomical location.
- The skeletal system includes bones plus associated cartilages and ligaments. Its functions depend on interactions with muscular, circulatory, endocrine, and other systems.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Make a function–anatomical example–system partner table. Connect vertebrae to protection, femur to support and movement, and marrow to blood-cell formation.

**Memory cue:** “Support Protects Movers; Minerals, Blood, Fat” recalls support, protection, movement, mineral storage, hematopoiesis, and fat storage.

**Precision check:** Bone is living, vascular tissue. A skeleton is not merely a static framework of mineral.

## 6.2 Bone Classification

[OpenStax section 6.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-2-bone-classification)

- Long bones are longer than wide and often act as levers, such as the femur and phalanges. Classification describes shape rather than absolute size.
- Short bones are roughly cube-shaped, such as most carpals and tarsals. Flat bones provide broad protective or attachment surfaces, as in sternum and cranial vault.
- Irregular bones have complex shapes, such as vertebrae. Sesamoid bones form within tendons and alter force transmission; the patella is the largest example.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Create a five-shape table with definition, two examples where available, and functional relationship. Sort a mixed list of bones without looking at the table.

**Memory cue:** “Long Shorts Fit Irregular Shapes” = Long, Short, Flat, Irregular, Sesamoid. A tiny phalanx is still a long bone.

**Precision check:** A flat bone can be curved. “Long bone” does not mean every physically large bone belongs in that category.

## 6.3 Bone Structure

[OpenStax section 6.3](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-3-bone-structure)

- A long bone has diaphysis, epiphyses, metaphyses, articular cartilage, medullary cavity, periosteum, and endosteum. Bone markings include attachment projections, joint surfaces, and passageways.
- Osteogenic cells give rise to osteoblasts. Osteoblasts produce matrix; osteocytes maintain it; osteoclasts resorb it. Organic collagen contributes tensile strength, while inorganic hydroxyapatite contributes hardness.
- Compact bone commonly contains osteons with concentric lamellae around central canals. Osteocytes occupy lacunae and communicate through canaliculi; perforating canals connect vascular routes.
- Spongy bone consists of trabeculae aligned with mechanical demands. Marrow occupies spaces between trabeculae. Nutrient vessels supply living bone cells.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw a long bone and enlarged osteon, then compare compact and spongy tissue. Make a bone-marking glossary with one real example per marking.

**Memory cue:** BLASTs Build; CLASTs Clear; CYTEs Care. Lacunae are little rooms; canaliculi connect them.

**Precision check:** Hydroxyapatite is inorganic mineral. Spongy bone is mineralized tissue with spaces, not soft unmineralized bone.

## 6.4 Bone Formation and Development

[OpenStax section 6.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-4-bone-formation-and-development)

- Intramembranous ossification forms bone within mesenchymal tissue. Endochondral ossification replaces a hyaline cartilage model. Most limb bones use the latter pathway.
- Primary ossification develops in the diaphysis; secondary centers develop in epiphyses. Articular cartilage remains at joint surfaces, and an epiphyseal plate remains during growth.
- OpenStax describes growth-plate zones of reserve cartilage, proliferation, maturation and hypertrophy, and calcified matrix. Cartilage grows toward the epiphyseal side while bone replaces it toward the diaphyseal side.
- Appositional growth increases diameter. Remodeling couples bone resorption and formation throughout life; closure of growth plates ends normal longitudinal growth.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw side-by-side ossification timelines and an enlarged growth plate oriented from epiphysis to diaphysis. Show periosteal deposition and endosteal resorption during widening.

**Memory cue:** “Resting People Mature and Calcify” = Reserve, Proliferation, Maturation/hypertrophy, Calcification. “Intra in membrane; Endo replaces cartilage.”

**Precision check:** Cartilage does not simply turn into bone cell-for-cell. The cartilage model is replaced by newly formed osseous tissue.

## 6.5 Fractures: Bone Repair

[OpenStax section 6.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-5-fractures-bone-repair)

- Classify fractures by pattern and relationship to skin: transverse, oblique, spiral, comminuted, impacted, greenstick, open, and closed. Several descriptors can apply to one fracture.
- Repair commonly proceeds through a hematoma, a fibrocartilaginous or soft callus, a bony or hard callus, and remodeling. Osteoblasts and osteoclasts participate at different stages.
- Reduction restores alignment; immobilization helps maintain conditions for healing. Open reduction and an open fracture describe different things.
- Healing rate and outcome depend on factors including blood supply, stability, extent of injury, nutrition, and age.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw four fracture-repair panels with tissue type and participating cells. Sketch each fracture pattern from the textbook and add one distinguishing sentence.

**Memory cue:** “Hematoma Softens; Bone Remodels” = Hematoma, Soft callus, Bony callus, Remodeling.

**Precision check:** A soft callus does not have the mechanical strength of mature remodeled bone. Open reduction refers to a surgical approach, not necessarily a fracture through skin.

## 6.6 Exercise, Nutrition, Hormones, and Bone Tissue

[OpenStax section 6.6](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-6-exercise-nutrition-hormones-and-bone-tissue)

- Bone adapts to mechanical loading. Deposition and resorption respond to stress; prolonged unloading can reduce bone mass.
- Calcium and phosphate provide mineral components. Vitamin D supports intestinal calcium absorption; vitamin C supports collagen formation. Protein supplies material for the organic matrix.
- Growth hormone, thyroid hormone, and sex hormones influence bone growth and maturation. Sex hormones contribute to the growth spurt and growth-plate closure.
- PTH, calcitriol, and calcitonin influence calcium regulation. Osteoporosis involves reduced bone mass and structural strength; it differs from defective mineralization.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Make a factor–target–effect table for mechanical load, nutrient roles, growth hormones, and calcium-regulating hormones. Compare osteoporosis with osteomalacia or rickets at the mechanism level.

**Memory cue:** Vitamin C builds Collagen; Vitamin D supports Digestive calcium uptake. “Load lays bone” recalls the response to appropriate mechanical demand.

**Precision check:** The study goal is understanding nutrient and hormone functions, not memorizing supplement doses. Bone quantity and mineralization quality are different variables.

## 6.7 Calcium Homeostasis: Interactions of the Skeletal System and Other Organ Systems

[OpenStax section 6.7](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/6-7-calcium-homeostasis-interactions-of-the-skeletal-system-and-other-organ-systems)

- Extracellular calcium supports neural signaling, muscle contraction, coagulation, and other functions. Bone is a large mineral reservoir, but blood calcium must remain within a narrow regulated range.
- Low blood calcium stimulates PTH release. PTH promotes renal calcium conservation, supports calcitriol formation, and increases bone resorption through signaling to osteoclast-regulating cells.
- Calcitriol increases intestinal calcium absorption. Calcitonin can inhibit osteoclast activity, although it has a smaller day-to-day role in adult calcium regulation than PTH and vitamin D.
- As calcium rises toward its regulated range, the low-calcium stimulus to PTH decreases. The loop links parathyroid glands, bone, kidneys, and intestine.

**Make these notes and drawings:** Draw a low-calcium negative-feedback loop with three target branches: bone, kidney, intestine via calcitriol. Use arrows to show calcium entering or leaving blood.

**Memory cue:** PTH = Puts calcium Higher. CalciTONin can Tone down blood calcium. Always attach the cue to a target organ and mechanism.

**Precision check:** PTH’s stimulation of osteoclast-mediated resorption is indirect. Blood calcium concentration is not a direct measure of total bone strength.
